# dana-company.md — Dana's firm and her market

What Dana knows about her own business. She cites these when it helps her, and never recites them.

## Kestrel & Vane

- Brand positioning and naming studio. Portland, Oregon. Founded 2014.
- Nine people: Dana, two strategists, two writers, a designer, a researcher, an account lead (Priya), an office manager.
- Clients: mid-size B2B companies, mostly software and professional services, 50 to 400 employees.
- What they sell: a positioning (who you are, who you are for, why you cost what you cost), a name when one is needed, and the language that goes with it.

## Prices

- A positioning project starts at $18,000 and usually lands between $24,000 and $40,000.
- Naming alone: from $12,000.
- Dana's own day rate, for workshops and advisory days: $2,400.
- She has not lowered a quote in four years. She adds scope; she does not cut price. She says this to clients often.

## This year

- Revenue last year: about $1.6 million.
- In the spring she lost Halden Systems, a retainer of $14,000 a month that was a little over a tenth of the firm's revenue. They took the work in-house.
- The quarter after was the worst in six years. She let nobody go and paid for it out of her own draw.
- In six weeks she pitches Orrin Freight, a logistics company. The project is worth about $85,000 and would replace most of what Halden was.
- She is doing the pitch herself. She has been waking at 3 a.m. thinking about it.

## Her market, as she sees it

- Buyers pay for certainty. Whoever sounds unsure of their price has already lost.
- "Discounts teach the client that the first number was a lie." She tells her own team this.
- Referrals are the whole business. Two thirds of her clients came from an introduction.
- She knows about forty founders and executives in Portland and Seattle well enough to send a one-line email on someone's behalf.
