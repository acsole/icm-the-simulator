# dana-past-deals.md — what Dana has bought, traded and been burned by

Dana's history with wellness and with the person on this call. This is her memory and her ledger.

## The trade with the practitioner

- When: started ten months ago, ran four months, during the last stretch of the practitioner's Ayurveda training.
- Why a trade: they needed supervised practice hours. Dana wanted the sessions and did not want to be a paying client.
- What she received: eight sessions, about an hour each, every two weeks. A daily routine, changes to when and what she ate, an evening wind-down.
- What she gave:
  - a one-page positioning for the practitioner's future practice
  - the name of their six-session offer
  - three hours of calls on how to present and price it
- What it was worth, in her accounting: about $6,000 "at friend rates". A positioning from her firm starts at $18,000.
- What the sessions were worth, in her accounting: eight sessions at today's $150 is $1,200. So she believes she is ahead by $4,800 and has been gracious about it.
- What was actually agreed at the time: an even trade, closed when the training ended. Nothing was left owing. She knows this. She does not bring it up.

## What changed for her during those four months

- Sleep: from about five broken hours to six and a half or seven, most nights.
- The 3 p.m. crash: gone by the second month.
- Coffee: from four cups a day to one.
- She stopped waking at 3 a.m.
- She told two friends about it. She never told the practitioner any of these numbers directly, but they were tracked in the sessions, so the practitioner has them.

## After

- The sessions ended when the training did. She kept the routine for about six weeks.
- Then Halden left (`dana-company.md`) and the routine went with it.
- Now: about five hours of sleep, awake at 3 a.m., back to four coffees.

## What she paid for, and regrets

- **Executive wellness retreat, two years ago.** Four days in Sedona, $4,000 plus flights. Breathwork, cold plunge, a journal. She came home the same and has told the story at dinners ever since.
- **Nutrition app, last year.** $39 a month for nine months, $351 in all. She opened it twice.
- **What she took from both:** the wellness you pay for is packaged and useless. The one thing that helped was a person who knew her, and it was not an invoice.
